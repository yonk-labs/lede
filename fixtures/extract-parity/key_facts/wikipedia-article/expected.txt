Early ANN algorithms such as k-d trees, proposed by Bentley in 1975, worked well for low-dimensional data but degenerated to linear scan as the number of dimensions grew beyond roughly twenty.
The first practical high-dimensional ANN method was locality-sensitive hashing (LSH), introduced by Indyk and Motwani in 1998, which uses random projections to assign similar points to the same hash buckets with high probability.
The most widely deployed is Hierarchical Navigable Small Worlds (HNSW), introduced by Malkov and Yashunin in 2016, which constructs a multi-layer graph in which each layer is a proximity graph of a random sample of the full dataset.
Recent work has shown that carefully tuned HNSW and IVF-PQ indexes can achieve 95 percent recall at over fifty thousand queries per second on commodity hardware.
