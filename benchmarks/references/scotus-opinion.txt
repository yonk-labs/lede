The Supreme Court in Northern Pacific Merchants Association v. EPA (No. 24-782, opinion by Justice Ramirez) addressed whether Section 412(b) of the Clean Water Enforcement Act permits EPA to impose civil penalties for discharges that comply with an unmodified NPDES permit but exceed effluent limits adopted by a subsequent rulemaking. The case arose from Cascade Processing LLC's 2019 permit authorizing 18 tons per year of dissolved metals, after which EPA's 2022 rule reduced the basin-wide limit to 11 tons and EPA sought penalties for the overage even though Cascade stayed within its permit ceiling. The Ninth Circuit allowed retroactive enforcement; the Supreme Court reversed. Holding: §412(b) authorizes penalties only for violations of permit terms as issued, and nothing in the text converts a subsequent rule into a retroactive permit term. The judgment of the Ninth Circuit is reversed and remanded.
