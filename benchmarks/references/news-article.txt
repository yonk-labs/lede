The European Central Bank held interest rates steady on Tuesday despite pressure from member states, citing stubborn services-sector inflation above target even as goods inflation cools. President Lagarde said the bank would remain data-dependent and declined to commit to a rate path. Markets responded modestly: the euro strengthened 0.4 percent against the dollar, European equities closed slightly lower, and bond yields rose with the German two-year up 8 basis points. Analysts are split — Goldman Sachs still expects a first cut in September while Morgan Stanley pushed its forecast to December; both agree the ECB will not move before the Federal Reserve. Member state reactions were divided: France and Italy called the stance too restrictive, while Germany warned against premature easing. The next policy decision is in July, which analysts view as the earliest credible window for a cut.
