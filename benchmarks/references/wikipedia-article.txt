Approximate nearest neighbor (ANN) search is a class of algorithms that finds points in a high-dimensional vector space close to a query point without guaranteeing exact results, trading small accuracy losses for large query-time and memory improvements. ANN is foundational for information retrieval, recommendation systems, and retrieval-augmented generation over embedding indexes of millions to billions of vectors. Historically, k-d trees (Bentley, 1975) worked only in low dimensions; the first practical high-dimensional method was locality-sensitive hashing (Indyk and Motwani, 1998). Today graph-based indexes dominate — especially Hierarchical Navigable Small Worlds (HNSW, Malkov and Yashunin, 2016), which achieves logarithmic query complexity and is shipped in FAISS, the pgvector Postgres extension, and nearly every production vector database. Performance is measured via recall at k and queries per second, benchmarked by the ANN-Benchmarks suite; tuned HNSW and IVF-PQ implementations reach 95 percent recall above 50,000 queries per second on commodity hardware.
